Feeding · 1–8 years

Picky eating: calm strategies that work

By the iChild.org Editorial Team · Reviewed against public pediatric guidance · Updated 2026-11-18 · 5 min read

Why young children are choosy about food, the division of responsibility, and practical ways to widen your child's diet without battles.

Picky eating is common — and usually temporary

Many toddlers and preschoolers go through phases of refusing new foods (food neophobia). Growth slows after the first year, so appetites naturally shrink and vary from day to day. Most picky eaters grow and develop normally.

Share the responsibility

A widely used approach is the division of responsibility: parents decide what, when and where food is offered; children decide whether to eat and how much. Removing pressure lowers stress and, over time, increases willingness to try.

Practical strategies

  • Serve a "safe" food alongside new ones at each meal so there's always something your child will eat.
  • Repeat exposure: it may take 10–15 tastes before a child accepts a new food. Keep offering small amounts without comment.
  • Family meals: eat together and enjoy a variety of foods yourself.
  • Predictable schedule: three meals and two to three snacks, with water in between, so kids come to the table hungry.
  • Get them involved: washing, stirring, choosing a vegetable at the store.
  • Skip the bribes: "eat your broccoli and you get dessert" teaches that vegetables are a chore and dessert the prize.
  • Serve family-style so children can choose portions.

When to get help

Talk to your doctor if your child is losing weight or falling off their growth curve, eats fewer than about 20 foods total, gags or vomits with new textures, has meals that cause extreme distress, or if eating is affecting family life significantly. Feeding therapists can help.

Important: This guide is general education, not medical advice. Every child is different — talk with your pediatrician, family doctor or child-health nurse about your child's specific needs.
