Safety · 0–5 years

Childproofing your home: a room-by-room checklist

By the iChild.org Editorial Team · Reviewed against public pediatric guidance · Updated 2026-11-18 · 7 min read

Practical, prioritized steps to prevent the most common home injuries — falls, poisoning, burns, drowning and furniture tip-overs.

Start before they move

Babies go from rolling to crawling to climbing faster than most parents expect. Childproof before your baby is mobile — ideally by 6 months — and re-check every few months as new skills appear. Get down on the floor at your child's eye level; you'll spot hazards you'd never see standing.

Whole-house priorities

  • Anchor furniture and TVs to the wall. Tip-overs injure thousands of children each year.
  • Lock up poisons: medicines, vitamins, cleaners, laundry pods, alcohol, e-cigarette liquids. Up high and locked.
  • Button batteries and magnets can cause severe internal injuries within hours if swallowed. Secure remote and toy battery compartments.
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms on every level; test monthly.
  • Window safety: window guards or stops that limit opening to 10 cm (4 in); move furniture away from windows; choose cordless blinds.
  • Stair gates at the top (hardware-mounted) and bottom of stairs.
  • Save your poison control number in your phone.

Kitchen

  • Cook on back burners and turn pot handles inward.
  • Use cabinet and oven locks; keep knives and dishwasher pods out of reach.
  • Never hold a child while handling hot drinks or cooking.

Bathroom

  • Never leave a child alone in the bath, even for a moment. Children can drown in a few centimeters of water.
  • Set your water heater to 49 °C (120 °F) or lower to prevent scalds.
  • Toilet locks for curious toddlers; keep razors and medicines locked.

Nursery and bedrooms

  • A crib with a firm mattress and nothing else inside (see safe sleep).
  • Keep cords from monitors and blinds well out of reach.
  • Use a changing table with a strap, and always keep one hand on baby.

Living areas

  • Corner guards on sharp furniture, outlet covers, secure fireplace screens.
  • Check houseplants — some are toxic.
  • Keep small objects (coins, beads, balloon pieces) off the floor. If it fits through a toilet-paper tube, it's a choking hazard.

Outside

  • Four-sided pool fencing with a self-closing, self-latching gate.
  • Empty buckets and paddling pools after use.
  • Store garden chemicals and tools locked away.

Finally, check your baby gear against our live recall alerts.

Important: This guide is general education, not medical advice. Every child is different — talk with your pediatrician, family doctor or child-health nurse about your child's specific needs.
