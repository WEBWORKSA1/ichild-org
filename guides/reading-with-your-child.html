Reading with your child at every age: a practical guide

By the iChild.org Editorial Team · Reviewed against public pediatric guidance · Updated 2026-11-18 · 5 min read

Why shared reading is one of the most powerful things you can do, and age-by-age ways to make books a daily joy.

Why reading together matters

Shared reading builds vocabulary, listening, imagination, attention and the emotional bond between you and your child. Children who are read to regularly hear many more words — and more varied words — than they would in everyday conversation, giving them a head start for school.

Babies (0–12 months)

  • Choose board books with bold pictures, faces and textures.
  • It's fine if baby chews the book — that's exploring.
  • Point and name: "Look, a dog! Woof woof."
  • A few minutes at a time is plenty; read when baby is calm and alert.

Toddlers (1–3 years)

  • Read the same favorites again and again — repetition is how they learn.
  • Pause and let them fill in the rhyme or the last word.
  • Ask "Where's the cat?" and let them point. Then "What is the cat doing?"

Preschoolers (3–5 years)

  • Use dialogic reading: ask open questions, expand on answers, and connect the story to their life.
  • Run your finger under the words occasionally to show print goes left to right.
  • Play with sounds: "Cat and hat rhyme! What else rhymes?"

Early readers (5–8 years)

  • Take turns reading pages.
  • Keep reading aloud books above their reading level — comprehension grows faster than decoding.
  • Celebrate effort and let them choose — comics and nonfiction count.

Independent readers (8–12 years)

  • Keep a family reading time where everyone reads their own book.
  • Listen to audiobooks on car trips.
  • Talk about characters' choices — great for empathy and moral reasoning.

Make it a habit

Link reading to an existing routine, like bedtime or after breakfast. Keep books in every room and in the car. Visit your local library — it's free, and many offer story times. Join our Story Time Challenge for a chance to win a book bundle.

Important: This guide is general education, not medical advice. Every child is different — talk with your pediatrician, family doctor or child-health nurse about your child's specific needs.
